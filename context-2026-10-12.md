## Design decisions (as written by the team)

1. **Explore is a strong label.** We worried that participants would have a hard time finding and using the explore page without typographic styling, but Explore was the first click 45.0% of the time, and first click 46.9% of the time when a different first click was expected. Because of this, we will keep the label as-is.
2. **We provide filters, not interpretations.** We designed the tests to gather data on what filters people would apply to specific tasks. A success was marked when people chose at least all the filters we guessed they would, and partial success was marked when people chose 1 or more of the filters we guessed they would. Overall, there was a 47.5% "success" rate. Including partial successes, that number rose, but only to 68.8%. This shows that we cannot guess what people want when looking for more subjective categories, and we should keep our filters as objective as possible. When a subjective category like Style needs to be described, we allow multiple views, such as allowing one venue to have multiple styles.
3. **Specialty Stations should be moved out of Decor.** People had a hard time finding the cake table (only 5/8, even though the label pointed there ("delicious cake")), and one participant said that it didn't belong there. Additionally, although 6/8 participants opened the decor tab, only 3 ended up clicking on a "specialty station", signaling that although some of these things could be considered activities, they are viewed more as items, since they are under the "Decor" section. We will call this section "Other" for now, and it will include "Included" and "For Rent" tags, as well.
4. **Parking as a filter should be removed.** We noticed that while some participants found the "designated lot" option easily, 3 out of 8 participants (P3, P7, and P8) still started by clicking on Capacity before finding the parking option, whereas most of the others went straight to Explore. P5 and P6 even had a hard time thinking through what a designated lot could mean when it's the only option. We considered adding a slider so users could define how many parking stalls they wanted, but due to a lack of information, this is not possible (doing so would limit the amount of venues showing up to be 6 instead of 46). Because of this, we plan on removing the parking filter and will only include that information on the individual venue pages themselves, and let the user infer that for the most part, capacity = amount of parking area.
5. **Capacity label on homepage should be changed.** We intended for the "Capacity" label on the homepage to be for those who want a large capacity for more guests, but 7/8 participants of task 7 chose it, so we need to change the label. For now, we will change it to "Spacious Venues." We will change Budget to "Budget Venues" to match this.
6. **Photos visible on Explore page.** Without photos present in the wireframe, we couldn't test this, but multiple participants (P2, P3, P4) noted that photos would help them with the task, especially tasks 1 and 9, where the visual design of the venue was important to the decision. In this study, people relied on the description for visual elements, but in the real website, photos will make that process easier. We've decided to include photos of the front of each venue, 2-4 other photos that users can arrow through on the Explore page.
7. **Search bar should allow searching venue descriptions.** P3 mentioned that this would have helped him find certain venues, and we think it would be helpful as a way to present other information in a view without causing too much clutter in the filters page.

## Claude's review of the hierarchy

The decisions hold up against the data. These gaps were raised:

1. **Nav links clear filters.** The 2026-10-10 handoff found that clicking Location, Style or "Explore all venues" wiped the filters already set, which cost at least three runs their target (tasks 1 and 6). D1–D7 don't address it. The team resolved it under Q1 below.
2. **Classic vs Luxury (task 1, 13% success) and task 9 have no direct decision.** D6 photos may fix them; say so in the report rather than leaving it implied.
3. **"Other" is a weak label.** Generic labels tend to get skipped. The team chose to keep it (Q5).
4. **Search scope.** D7 says descriptions only. The team widened it (Q4).
5. **Decor and Other have separate Included / For rent toggles.** People may expect one toggle that covers both. Not yet decided.

All three drafts use the 46 real venues. Filters, counts, search, popular searches and photo arrows work. Photos are placeholder boxes, 3–5 per venue (front photo plus 2–4 more, per D6). Blue dashed tags mark where each of D1–D7 appears, and a toggle hides them. Each draft has a home page, Explore and a venue page. The venue page now carries parking information (D4).

## Team answers to the nine questions

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Should Location/Style add to existing filters or start fresh? | **Replace filters and show an Undo bar** (draft B behavior) |
| 2 | Which filter layout? | **Always-visible sidebar** (draft A) |
| 3 | How much photo per result card? | **Carousel on every card** |
| 4 | What should search match? | **Everything that has information.** Search every field with content; a blank field never produces a match (for example, if parking is blank, don't return results for it) |
| 5 | What to call "Other"? | **Keep "Other"** |
| 6 | Home page browse by Style with photos? | **Yes, a photo tile for each style** |
| 7 | Full venue page or preview? | **Full venue page always** |
| 8 | Phone or laptop first? | **Laptop first** |
| 9 | Where to build the next prototype? | **A Figma draft first**, then build on GitHub Pages from the Figma prototype |
